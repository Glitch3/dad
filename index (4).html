From lump to kiln

Every pot goes through the same six stages, and two trips through the fire. Scroll down to fire one.

  1. Wedging

    The clay is kneaded on the bench to push out air bubbles that would crack in the kiln.

  2. Throwing

    A lump is centred on the spinning wheel, opened up, and pulled into shape between Norman's hands.

  3. Trimming

    Once it's leather-hard, the pot goes back on the wheel upside down and a foot is carved into the base.

  4. Bisque firing

    A first firing to about 1000°C turns soft clay into hard, porous ceramic that can take glaze.

  5. Glazing

    Each piece is dipped or poured with glaze, much of it mixed by Norman from raw materials.

  6. Glaze firing

    The final firing to around 1280°C melts the glaze into glass and makes the pot strong, watertight and food safe.

About Norman

Norman Cowie works in two very different mediums: video and clay.

As a video artist, he has spent decades making documentaries and experimental videos that look at power and at how television and the news shape what we come to believe. His work includes Nazareth in August (1986), Lying in State (1989), About Face (1991), Signal to Noise: Life with Television (1996, co-directed) and Welcome to New York (2002). It often borrows straight from broadcast footage, cut together with sound and text.
